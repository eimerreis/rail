Retry budgets are smaller than you think

Published 30 October

Every retry policy I have inherited was written on a good day. Three attempts, exponential backoff, a little jitter. It reads as caution. Then the database slows down for ninety seconds and those three attempts become a second wave of traffic, aimed at the one thing that is already struggling.

The fix is rarely a smarter policy. It is a budget: a limit on how much extra load retries may add across the whole service, not per request.

Count what you can afford

Start with the spare capacity you actually have. If the ledger handles 400 reads a second and normally serves 340, you can afford 60 retries a second, and that number has to be shared.

retries_allowed = spare_capacity × window
# 10% of normal traffic is a sensible first limit

Once the budget runs out, callers fail fast and say so. That feels worse in the dashboard for a few minutes, and it is much better for everyone waiting in the queue behind them.
